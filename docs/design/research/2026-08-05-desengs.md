# Design Research: DesEngs

**Observed:** 2026-08-05
**Method:** Live site review plus inspection of the public source repository and
`src/data/resources.ts`.

## Executive Summary

DesEngs is a useful global discovery layer for work at the intersection of
design and implementation. Its strongest structural idea is an action-oriented
taxonomy: resources are grouped by what someone wants to do rather than by a
rigid design discipline. The local knowledge base adopts that source as a hub,
then adds project-delivery relationships that DesEngs itself does not claim.

## Observations

- The site describes itself as a resource curated by design engineers for design
  engineers.
- It exposes ten formats: Read, Watch, Listen, Browse, Use, Build, Learn, Join,
  Follow, and Apply.
- The inspected source snapshot contains 77 resource entries. Read, Browse, Use,
  Build, Learn, Join, Follow, and Apply are populated; Watch and Listen are not.
- Each resource has a date, format, title, URL, and optional description.
- The underlying site is public and open source; resource additions are requested
  through GitHub issues.

## Interpretation

- The action taxonomy is a good entrypoint for exploratory design research
  because it starts with intent: learn a concept, browse patterns, use a tool, or
  build a component.
- The collection is especially relevant to design engineering, UI craft,
  interaction, motion, design systems, and agent-assisted frontend work.
- DesEngs should remain a discovery source. Its inclusion decisions do not prove
  accessibility, maintainability, licensing fitness, or suitability for a
  specific product.

## Applied Locally

- Added DesEngs and its source repository to the design source registry.
- Seeded five theme clusters rather than copying all entries.
- Created a graph from source hub to resource format, design question, project
  artifact, verification, and feedback.
- Added an authority rule that product requirements and project design sources
  take precedence over external inspiration.

## Questions For Further Research

- Which DesEngs resources repeatedly improve real project outcomes?
- Which references cover accessibility and inclusive design deeply enough to add
  as core sources?
- Should a future index capture screenshots or metadata snapshots when linked
  resources are likely to disappear?
- Which graph edges should be automated, and which require human design judgment?

## Limitations

- The 77 linked resources were not individually reviewed.
- Counts and links can change after the observation date.
- The site's curation criteria and coverage biases are not fully documented.
- The local theme clusters are interpretation, not DesEngs's published taxonomy.
